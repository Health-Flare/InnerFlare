# Inner Flare 1.1.0: release notes

Release notes covering everything that's landed since
[v1.0.1](https://github.com/Health-Flare/InnerFlare/releases/tag/v1.0.1):
Quick Stats, the unlock screen rework ([PR #19](https://github.com/Health-Flare/InnerFlare/pull/19)),
symptom tracking settings, backup export/import, and a calendar edit-refresh
fix, all merged to `main`.

These are additive feature/UX changes, not a breaking change to anything
external, so this ships as a minor version (`1.1.0`) rather than `2.0.0`.

## Full release notes

### A calmer way to unlock Inner Flare

Opening Inner Flare (for the first time, or any time after stepping away)
used to be able to jump straight to your device's raw "Enter Passcode"
prompt before the app had shown you anything of its own. That's fixed:

- A single screen now greets you first, every time, explaining plainly that
  your cycle data is encrypted on your device and needs Face ID, Touch ID,
  or your passcode to view. Nothing about your data is touched until you
  tap **Unlock**.
- First open and returning after being away now look and feel like the same
  moment, instead of two different mechanics.
- If an unlock attempt is cancelled or fails, you get a clear explanation
  and a single button to try again, never a repeat prompt you didn't ask for.

Nothing about *what's* protected has changed: your data was, and still is,
encrypted at rest and gated behind your device's own biometrics. This is
purely about *how it asks*.

### Quick Stats on your dashboard

Two configurable numbers now sit right below your daily log:

- **Days since your last period**: measured from either the start or the
  end of your last logged period, whichever matches how you think about it.
- **Estimated days to your next period**: a plain-statistics estimate
  based on your own logged history, with an honest "not enough data yet"
  when there isn't enough to go on.

Both are reconfigurable from the dashboard, so you're not stuck with
defaults that don't match how you track.

### Choose exactly which symptoms you track

Settings now has a "Symptoms to track" screen:

- Turn any default symptom off if you never log it: it disappears from the
  daily log screen without touching any past entry that already used it.
- Add your own custom symptoms, enabled by default the moment you create
  them.
- Re-enabling a symptom brings it straight back to the log screen.

### Back up and restore your data

A new Export/Import screen (in Settings) lets you save an encrypted copy of
your cycle data and settings to a file you choose, and load it back in,
onto the same phone after a reinstall, or a new one:

- Export is a deliberate, explicit action: nothing leaves the device
  automatically or without you choosing a destination.
- Import is designed to round-trip cleanly from older app versions, so a
  backup made today will still restore correctly after future updates.
- Per-device settings (dashboard layout, Quick Stats choices, your idle-lock
  timeout) are intentionally left out of the backup: they come back from
  each device's own settings, not from the file.

### Fixed: calendar edit-refresh bug

Editing a previous day from the calendar could show a false "Couldn't save"
error even though the edit had actually saved. That's fixed: the calendar
now refreshes correctly after an edit to a past day.

### Under the hood

A handful of routine dependency updates (Gradle, Android Gradle Plugin, the
Kotlin Android plugin, `desugar_jdk_libs`), no user-visible change, kept
here for the technically curious and for F-Droid's changelog.
